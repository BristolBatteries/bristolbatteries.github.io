Amey is conducting a PhD on Characterising heterogeneous battery operation and its effect on pack configuration, thermal management and degradation for ultra-high power applications. He aims to identify how thermal heterogeneity propagates up length scales and determine a method to implement its effects into simplistic models. Amey also aims to investigate the coupling between cell parametrisation and its model application use case.

Lumped battery models, commonly implemented in Battery Management Systems (BMSs) and used in academia, use a single temperature to estimate the parameters of the cell at each time step. This is not representative of the behaviour of a battery as, depending on the type of thermal management implemented, thermal gradients develop over the cell. The problem of thermal heterogeneity is not captured in these models due to its ‘idealistic’ cell temperature. Therefore, state estimation errors arise as true temperature of the cell is not represented by the single state predicted by the model.

By capturing the effects of this heterogeneity within battery models across length scales, we can identify the pack configurations and thermal management strategies that minimise its effect. This would also allow for increasingly accurate performance predictions, vital for stakeholders across industry.
