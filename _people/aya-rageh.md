Aya is conducting a PhD on how Sodium-ion (Na-ion) batteries could provide a more sustainable alternative to Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) batteries for electric vehicles. Her research investigates not only the environmental impact of producing, using, and recycling these batteries, but also how their performance changes under different temperatures. By combining battery ageing experiments with life cycle assessment (LCA), her work provides a more realistic picture of long-term battery sustainability, including how both battery capacity and power capability degrade over time.

A major focus of her research is understanding how geography influences the sustainability of Na-ion batteries. Unlike lithium resources, which are concentrated in a small number of countries, the materials used in Na-ion batteries are more widely available, meaning production could be distributed across many different regions worldwide. This makes local factors, particularly electricity generation, far more important in determining environmental impact. Her research therefore examines how different energy systems, from fossil fuel–based grids to renewable electricity networks, influence emissions throughout the battery life cycle, while also considering how climate conditions affect battery degradation and lifetime.

Using a modified Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicles Test Cycle (WLTC), Aya compares the ageing behaviour and environmental performance of Na-ion and LFP batteries under different temperatures by analysing both capacity fade and power fade. The aim is to create a direct comparison between the two technologies while showing how local climate and energy infrastructure can influence their overall sustainability. This research supports the development of more environmentally informed battery technologies and future electric vehicle strategies.
