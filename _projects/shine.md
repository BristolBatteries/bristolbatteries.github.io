## Overview

The Ayrton-funded project investigates how energy access interventions can be made more inclusive, sustainable and beneficial to communities. Working with communities and stakeholders in Ghana and The Gambia, SHINE explores the social, economic, gender, environmental, health and technological factors that shape the energy transition. The consortium includes partners from The Gambia, Ghana, South Africa, Brazil, Nepal and the UK.  

Within SHINE, our work focuses on the role of stationary battery energy storage in off-grid energy systems. Battery models are being developed and deployed to represent how storage systems behave under realistic operating conditions, including variable electricity demand for off-grid communities and changing power input from solar arrays. This modelling provides a way to explore how battery systems are used, stressed and degraded in practice, and how their performance depends on local patterns of energy demand and renewable generation.

By linking battery behaviour to real community energy-use profiles, this work supports better design and assessment of solar-plus-storage systems for energy access applications. The aim is to help ensure that storage systems are appropriately sized, reliable and sustainable over their lifetime, while providing technical evidence that can support wider decisions about inclusive and resilient clean energy deployment in Ghana, The Gambia and similar off-grid contexts.
