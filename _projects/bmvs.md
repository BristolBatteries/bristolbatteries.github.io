## Overview

This Faraday Institution Sprint project, delivered in collaboration with About:Energy, developed the foundations for more robust and standardised battery model validation methods. Battery models are widely used to support the design and operation of electric vehicles, energy storage systems and other electrified technologies, but confidence in these models depends on how they are validated. This project focused on making validation more transparent, reproducible and aligned with real engineering use cases, rather than relying only on single headline performance metrics.

A key focus of the work was the thermal environment used during battery validation experiments. The project showed that the way a cell is thermally controlled during testing can have a major effect on the data recorded, and therefore on the apparent performance of a battery model. Initial experiments compared cells tested in a conventional climate chamber with cells tested in more application-relevant, base-cooled configurations. These results highlighted large differences in the effective heat transfer conditions experienced by the cell, demonstrating that validation results can depend strongly on the experimental thermal setup.

The project provided early evidence that battery model validation standards must include clear characterisation of the test environment, particularly thermal boundary conditions. This work has informed subsequent research on standardising battery model validation, including how to define validation data quality, how to compare models fairly across different experimental conditions, and how to reduce thermal error effects in battery model assessment.

Overall, the project helped establish a clearer route towards battery model validation methods that are reproducible, use-case aligned and trusted by both academic and industrial users.
